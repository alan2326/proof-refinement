## Mathematical insight (required, ≤ 5 sentences)

The Cr_phi formula at V9 is `Cr_phi = M_phi · M_phi_prime · invFp² + (4R/(1-r)²) · M_phi²` where `invFp := 2R/(1-r) ≈ 28.75` is the *paper's worst-case* upper bound on `1/‖D‖` derived via `F_p_deriv_lower_bound_at_F_inv : ‖D‖ ≥ (1-r)/(2R)`; this paper bound proves `‖D‖` is non-zero but is enormously loose at our η = 1/100 because `D = (1-p)·H_0' + p·H_η'` is dominated by `H_0' = 1/√(1-w²)` (since `H_0(w) = arcsin(w)`, per `LeanProof/Defs/Kernel.lean:38-59`). On the oracle rectangle `Ω(δ,B) = {|Re w| < 1-δ, |Im w| < B}` with δ = 1635/10000, B = 1115/1000, the actual `inf |H_0'(w)| = 1/sqrt(sup|1-w²|) ≈ 0.643` and the η/p perturbation adds at worst `p_max · M_val ≈ 2.4·10⁻⁴ · 428 ≈ 0.103`, giving `inf |D| ≥ 0.540` and hence `sup 1/|D| ≤ 1.854`. The new oracle `inv_F_p_z` certifies this via arb on the rectangle (closed-form: at the corner `w = (1-δ) + iB` the supremum is attained, and arb-certification is direct — the bound depends only on the rectangle dimensions, not on `η` directly except through the small p·M perturbation). At `inv_F_p_z_val := 2` (rounded up from 1.854), the new term1 = `M_phi · M_phi_prime · 4 ≈ 0.0104` (down from `2.145`), and Cr_phi drops from `3.79` to `1.65` — see `Scratch/sprint_014_planner_invFp_oracle.py` for the full numerical verification.

## Why (≤ 3 sentences, must include numerical evidence)

`Scratch/sprint_014_planner_invFp_oracle.py` (output: `inf |H_0'| ≈ 0.643`, `sup 1/|H_0'| ≈ 1.556`; with η/p perturbation at `p_max ≈ 2.4·10⁻⁴` and `M_val = 428`, `inf|D| ≈ 0.540`, `sup 1/|D| ≈ 1.854`; Cr_phi at `invFp = 2` becomes `0.0104 + 1.6422 = 1.6526`, leaving margin `0.347` for `Cr_val := 2` under `nlinarith`). The 2.5× win over V9's `Cr_val = 5` is sourced entirely from term1, which currently dominates Cr_phi (term1 ≈ 2.14 vs term2 ≈ 1.64); future sprints can target term2 separately. The oracle's certification path is direct: `1/|D(η,p,w)|^2 = 1/((1-p)·H_0'(w) + p·H_η'(w))^2` is a sup over a rectangle with smooth integrand, certifiable by arb's grid-or-Cauchy-bound machinery exactly like `compute_M_phi_bound`.
