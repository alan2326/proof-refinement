\noindent\textit{Companion document to the main paper.} This appendix
describes the multi-agent harness that drove the certified upper bound on
$\KG$ from the Braverman~et~al.\ baseline ($\KG \le \pi/(2\ln(1+\sqrt2))-3\!\cdot\!10^{-32}$)
to the present $\KG \le \pi/(2\ln(1+\sqrt2))-10^{-17}$. The harness is
file-based, agentic, and structured around a small number of explicit
guarantees about who may write what. We describe the components in the
order they fire during a single iteration of the loop.

\bigskip

%======================================================================
\section{Goal and game}
%======================================================================

Lemma~5.4 of \cite{braverman2011} bounds the second-order Taylor remainder
of an implicit-function family at $p=0$ by $\Cr\, p^2$. The constant
$\Cr$ propagates verbatim into the certified $\KG$-upper-bound:
shrinking $\Cr$ shrinks the gap to Krivine.

The harness curates a sequence of \emph{candidates}
$V_0, V_1, V_2,\dots,V_N$, each a self-contained Lean~4 file in
\code{ProofRefinement/Lemma54/Candidates/} that proves Lemma~5.4 with a
declared rational $\Cval_K$. The seed $V_0$ mirrors a paper-faithful
production proof; later candidates explore departures. The harness's only
goal is
\[
   V_{N+1} \text{ exists, compiles, audits clean, and has } \Cval_{N+1} < \Cval_N.
\]
Strictly smaller score wins. Everything else---formalization style,
oracle technology, formula choice---is means.

\section{The artifacts (file-based state)}
%======================================================================

The harness has no in-memory state. Every fact that survives across
agent invocations lives in one of these files. The table below is the
complete enumeration; nothing else persists.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.42\textwidth}p{0.52\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Path} & \textbf{Purpose} \\
\midrule
\code{Candidates/V<K>.lean}                        & Append-only sequence of Lean candidates. $V_0$ is the seed. \\
\code{Sprint_Contracts/sprint_<NNN>.md}            & One contract per planned sprint, written by the \Role{Planner}. Append-only. \\
\code{Refinement_Log/log.jsonl}                    & Append-only JSONL: one line per evaluator run (compile, axiom audit, score, oracle results, timing). \\
\code{Refinement_Log/verdict_<NNN>.md}             & Markdown verdict file written by the \Role{Evaluator} per sprint. \\
\code{Bulletin.md}                                 & Volatile signaling between sprints ($\le$100 lines, trimmed). The current priority directive lives here. \\
\code{Experience.md}                               & Narrative agent log: what was tried, what worked, what didn't. Written by the \Role{Generator}. \\
\code{Scratch/sprint_<NNN>_<topic>.{py,md,txt}}    & Persistent research notebook. Numerical experiments by all roles. \\
\code{Proposals/proposal_<NNN>_<slug>.md}          & Human-approval queue for non-oracle trust expansions. Status: \code{pending}/\code{approved}/\code{rejected}. \\
\code{lemma.toml}                                  & Single source of truth for the sink theorem, the score expression, the seed-axiom allowlist, and the oracle table. \\
\code{PATCH_NOTES.md}                              & Binding-rule document for any rule introduced retroactively (e.g., the $K_{\max}$-truncation regression of 2026-05-03). \\

\code{Numerical_Library/}                          & Python package of certified-arb oracle implementations, indexed by \code{lemma.toml}'s \code{[[oracles]]} table. \\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The discipline is exact: every other file is read-only or absent. In
particular there is no shared in-memory orchestrator; each agent invocation
is a fresh process that reads files, writes files, and exits.

%======================================================================
\section{The three roles}
%======================================================================

The harness consists of three agent roles, each defined by a Markdown
prompt in \code{.claude/agents/}. They are spawned sequentially, never
concurrently, and never co-resident in the same process.

\subsection{The \Role{Planner}}

\paragraph{Inputs.} Bulletin, Experience, the last 10 log entries, the
last contract, the production trust-boundary status, the agent brief,
all proposals (just their \code{Status} lines), and PATCH\_NOTES.

\paragraph{Output.} \emph{Exactly one} Sprint Contract file
\code{Sprint_Contracts/sprint_<NNN>.md} per invocation, plus optional
Bulletin appends and Scratch/ exploration scripts.

\paragraph{Discipline.} The Planner never edits Lean. Its sole
deliverable is a contract that prescribes what edits the
\Role{Generator} should make. It must (i) work out the right move via
\code{Scratch/} numerical experiments before drafting (\emph{Scratch-first}),
(ii) not repeat the same direction more than three sprints in a row
(\emph{no-same-axis-three-times}), and (iii) schedule a wildcard sprint
branched from $V_0$ at least every fourth sprint
(\emph{forced trajectory escape}).

\paragraph{Sprint Contract structure.} A contract is a Markdown file
with a fixed set of fields:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Parent candidate.} Which $V_K$ the new candidate branches
        from (typically $V_{N-1}$, but explicit non-default parents are
        permitted and required for wildcard sprints).
  \item \textbf{Target file.} The new candidate path
        $V_{N+1}\code{.lean}$ (or, rarely, a research script under
        \code{Scratch/}).
  \item \textbf{Direction.} A short tag such as
        \code{formula-change}, \code{contour-change}, \code{inline-oracle},
        \code{oracle-resolution-tightening}, \code{research}.
  \item \textbf{Tier.} \code{cheap} (≤ 30~min, surgical) or
        \code{ambitious} (≤ 3~h, substantial new Lean).
  \item \textbf{Goal} (one declarative sentence).
  \item \textbf{Mathematical insight} (≤ 5 sentences). What
        inequality, identity, or proof-method shift is being attempted,
        with citations to \code{Proof/Lib/Lemma54_Paper*.lean} or to the
        paper.
  \item \textbf{Why} (≤ 3 sentences with numerical evidence). Cite a
        \code{Scratch/sprint_<NNN>_planner_<topic>.py} run that supports
        the proposed move.
  \item \textbf{Concrete edits expected.} The exhaustive list of files
        the Generator may write to. Anything else is a write-scope
        violation (gate~G3, below).
  \item \textbf{Acceptance criteria.} 2–5 mechanically checkable
        \code{bash} / \code{jq} / \code{grep} predicates --- each one
        either an exit code or a \code{log.jsonl} field comparison.
  \item \textbf{Out of scope.} Explicit no-touch list, beyond the
        always-forbidden defaults.
  \item \textbf{Time budget} and \textbf{Stop conditions for the
        Generator.} Including the labeled status sentinels
        \code{STATUS=READY_FOR_EVALUATION}, \code{STATUS=BLOCKED},
        \code{STATUS=BLOCKED_NEEDS_HUMAN}.
\end{itemize}

A typical contract is 2–6 pages, with the bulk of the content in
\textbf{Mathematical insight} and \textbf{Concrete edits}. The
contract is the harness's \emph{atomic unit of work}: one contract
$\to$ one Generator session $\to$ one Evaluator session $\to$ one
verdict $\to$ at most one new candidate.

\subsection{The \Role{Generator}}

\paragraph{Inputs.} The contract path it was given; the parent
candidate; the brief; PATCH\_NOTES; the specific files the contract
names.

\paragraph{Output.} The new candidate file (or research script);
optional Edits to the files the contract names; an Experience.md
append; a labeled terminating \code{STATUS=} sentinel.

\paragraph{Discipline.} The Generator never grades its own work, never
edits the contract, never edits older candidates, never edits the
Evaluator or runner code, never adds a new (non-oracle) axiom. The
write-scope is gated mechanically: only the files in the contract's
\textbf{Concrete edits expected} list, plus three always-allowed paths
(\code{Experience.md}, \code{Bulletin.md} (one-line append),
\code{Scratch/}). Everything else is a write-scope violation.

\paragraph{Inline oracle creation.} A central feature: if the
mathematical move requires a new sup-norm bound on an analytic
expression over the oracle rectangle $\Omega(\delta,B)$, the Generator
mints the oracle \emph{in the same sprint}, without going through the
proposal queue. The recipe is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
  \item Write \code{Numerical_Library/m_<name>.py} computing
        $\sup_{w\in\Omega(\delta,B)}|\text{expr}(w)|$ via
        certified-arb interval arithmetic, returning an
        \code{OracleResult(bound: Fraction, ball_value: float, ...)}.
  \item Append a new \code{[[oracles]]} block to \code{lemma.toml}
        with the axiom short name, the bound-field, and the
        \code{oracle_fn} attribute name.
  \item Declare a Lean axiom in the candidate of the form
        \(
          \forall p\in[0,1],\, \forall w\in\Omega(\delta,B),\,
          \|\text{expr}(p,w)\| \le \text{bound\_field\_val}
          \cdot \|D(p,w)\|^k,
        \)
        which the Generator then consumes in the proof.
\end{enumerate}
This is safe because the runtime arb pipeline re-verifies every oracle
on \emph{every} audit (gate~G5). A wrong Python implementation is
caught structurally by the bound-field-vs-recomputed-bound inequality
and content-wise by the human's morning-after diff review. Oracle-shaped
axioms cannot be used to ``prove'' arbitrary statements: their
syntactic form constrains them to numerical sup-norm claims.

\subsection{The \Role{Evaluator}}

\paragraph{Inputs.} The contract path; the candidate file; access to
\code{lake env lean} and \code{Numerical_Library}.

\paragraph{Output.} A Markdown verdict file
\code{Refinement_Log/verdict_<NNN>.md} and a single appended JSON line
to \code{log.jsonl}. Terminates with
\code{STATUS=VERDICT_PASS} or \code{STATUS=VERDICT_FAIL}.

\paragraph{Discipline.} The Evaluator never edits Lean, never argues
with a failing gate, never reads the Generator's narrative when
deciding pass/fail. Gates are binary.

\paragraph{Gates (in order, terminating on first failure).} The seven
gates are deliberately cheap. Aggregate runtime $\sim$30~s.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4em,style=nextline,itemsep=0.4em]
  \item[G1: Compile (focused).] After clearing stale \code{.olean},
        \code{lake env lean} the new candidate. Pass = exit 0, no
        \code{error:} lines.
  \item[G2: No \code{sorry}.] \code{grep} the candidate. Any match fails.
  \item[G3: Write scope.] \code{git diff --name-only HEAD} must be a
        subset of (contract-named files) $\cup$ (always-allowed paths)
        $\cup$ (a new $V_{N+1}$ file). Older candidates are sacrosanct.
  \item[G4: No new non-oracle axioms.] Compare the new candidate's
        \code{#print axioms} set to the parent's. New axioms are
        permitted only if their \code{kind = oracle} and they appear
        in \code{lemma.toml}'s \code{[[oracles]]} table. Seed or
        unregistered axiom additions fail with
        \code{NEW_AXIOM_INTRODUCED}.
  \item[G5: Per-candidate evaluator.] Run
        \code{Evaluator.py --file <cand>}. This recomputes every oracle
        bound by calling its registered \code{oracle_fn}, and checks
        \(
          \text{bound\_field\_val} \;\ge\; \text{oracle\_fn}().\text{bound}.
        \)
        Pass = exit 0 and \code{pass = true} in the JSON record.
  \item[G7: Rigor (anti-truncation + audit-floor).] Two parts; both
        must pass. \emph{G7.a} \code{grep}s the candidate and every
        oracle source it depends on for the four forbidden symbols
        (\code{eval_H_eta_at_acb}, \code{eval_phi_at_acb},
        \code{eval_H_eta_deriv_at_acb}, \code{eval_phi_and_deriv_at_acb}).
        These are the $K_{\max}$-truncated evaluators that admit no
        rigorous tail bound; any candidate whose oracle path reaches
        them is invalid by construction. \emph{G7.b} parses the
        candidate's mandatory audit-block header (PATCH\_NOTES Rule~R5),
        looks up the named row in the cached audit JSON, and checks
        \(
          \Cpatched_{\text{audit}} \;\le\; \Cval_{\text{declared}}.
        \)
        This binary reconciliation between the declared and the
        truncation-free-recomputed bound is the gate that would have
        caught the $K_{\max}$ regression had it existed earlier.
  \item[G6: Score.] Parse the just-appended \code{log.jsonl} line.
        Confirm $\Cval_{N+1} < \Cval_N$ (or whatever inequality the
        contract declared).
\end{description}

The order \emph{G5 $\to$ G7 $\to$ G6} is deliberate: a candidate that
has already passed the per-candidate evaluator should still be rejected
if its rigor floor is wrong, before its score is even compared.

%======================================================================
\section{The trust boundary}
%======================================================================

Every axiom transitively depended on by the sink theorem
\code{lemma_5_4_search} falls into exactly one of three classes,
recognized by \code{Evaluator.py} via \code{lemma.toml}.

\paragraph{Lean primitives.} \code{propext}, \code{Classical.choice},
\code{Quot.sound}. Always permitted.

\paragraph{Seed axioms.} A whitelist in
\code{lemma.toml:[seed_axioms.allowed]}. The 12 paper-cited analytic
axioms about the implicit-function family $F^{-1}_{\text{paper}}$
(\code{F_inv_paper}, \code{F_inv_paper_eq}, \code{F_inv_paper_zero},
\code{F_inv_paper_R_bound}, \code{F_inv_paper_analytic},
\code{F_inv_paper_contdiff}, \code{F_inv_paper_first_deriv_zero},
\code{F_inv_paper_mem_S}, \code{pr_paper}, \code{pr_paper_pos},
\code{pr_paper_le_one}, and the structural domain-containment
\code{h_oracle_domain_contains_image}) live here. Each represents a
fact the formalization takes on paper-faith.

\paragraph{Oracle axioms.} An array of \code{[[oracles]]} records, each
of which has the form
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small,xleftmargin=2em]
[[oracles]]
axiom       = "h_<name>_oracle"
bound_field = "<field>_val"
oracle_fn   = "compute_<name>_truncfree_bound"
\end{Verbatim}
The Lean axiom must be of the sup-norm shape
\(
  \forall p\in[0,1],\,\forall w\in\Omega(\delta,B),\,
  \|\text{expr}(p,w)\| \le \text{<field>\_val}\cdot \|D(p,w)\|^k.
\)
At every audit, the Evaluator runs the registered \code{oracle\_fn}
(certified arb computation) and rejects the candidate if the declared
\code{<field>\_val} fails to dominate the recomputed bound.

\paragraph{Anything else} (\code{sorryAx}, an unregistered axiom, an
oracle whose short-name is missing from \code{lemma.toml}) is a hard
fail.

\paragraph{Trust expansion.} The Generator may freely add an
oracle-shaped axiom by minting a new \code{[[oracles]]} block (Inline
oracle creation). It may not add anything else. To expand the
non-oracle trust base, see \S\ref{sec:proposals}.

%======================================================================
\section{The proposal queue (asynchronous human approval)}\label{sec:proposals}
%======================================================================

A proposal is the Planner's request for the human to expand the
non-oracle trust base. Trust-base expansions of the following kinds
require a proposal:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
  \item A new free-floating analytic axiom (e.g., a wider analyticity
        domain for $F^{-1}_{\text{paper}}$).
  \item A non-trivial Mathlib import bringing in disputed/heavy
        infrastructure.
  \item A statement reformulation (rare; typically forbidden as a
        weakening of Lemma~5.4).
\end{itemize}

A proposal is a Markdown file
\code{Proposals/proposal_<NNN>_<slug>.md} naming, in mechanically
actionable detail, the Lean axiom to add, the lemma.toml entry to
modify, the math content (with paper citation), the numerical win
unlocked (with a Scratch/ run cited), and the trust cost.

\paragraph{Async discipline.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
  \item The Planner files a proposal \emph{and queues a regular sprint
        in the same invocation} so the loop never stalls. The queued
        sprint must not depend on the pending proposal.
  \item Budget: at most 3 \code{pending} proposals at any time.
  \item The human reviews on their own time, edits the proposal's
        \code{Status:} field to \code{approved} or \code{rejected},
        appends a status-log line, and (if approved) lands the
        infrastructure (Lean axiom + lemma.toml entry).
  \item The Planner reads \code{Status: approved} on its next
        invocation and queues a sprint that consumes the new
        infrastructure.
\end{enumerate}

In our run the proposal queue surfaced one approved proposal
(\code{proposal_001_cr_refined_oracles}: three $\eta$-aware oracles
$M_\Phi, M_{\Phi'}, M_{\Phi\Phi}$ that unlocked the $\eta^2$-tight
$C_r^{\text{refined}}$ formula) and one pending one
(\code{proposal_003_wider_analyticity_domain}: a paper-citable wider
analyticity disk that would lift the Cauchy contour radius by~$1.5\times$).

\section{The numerical library and the certified-arb pipeline}
%======================================================================

\code{Numerical_Library/} is a Python package indexed by
\code{lemma.toml}'s oracle table. Each oracle module exposes a single
\code{compute_<name>_bound(...)} entry point that returns
\code{OracleResult(bound, ball_value, ...)}, where:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
  \item \code{ball_value} is a single \code{float} approximating the
        sup the oracle bounds;
  \item \code{bound} is a \code{Fraction} rationalized upward from the
        certified arb interval, with safety parameters
        \code{(safety_rel, safety_abs, max_denom)} producing a small
        but \emph{provably valid} excess over the arb upper bound.
\end{itemize}
The library wraps the lower-level certified-arb infrastructure at
\code{grothendieck/proof/certified/}, which is the same code base the
production proof-graph runner consumes (\S\ref{sec:proofgraph}).

\paragraph{Truncation-free oracles.} The 2026-05-03 patch
(PATCH\_NOTES.md) introduced
\code{Numerical_Library/truncfree/}, a parallel oracle suite that
evaluates $\Heta, \Heta', \Heta''$ via Price's-theorem path integrals
and chunk-bounds them via real-positive Gaussian majorants---no
$\phi$-series truncation anywhere in the call graph. The deprecated
top-level oracles are kept only to satisfy backwards-compat for older
candidates; G7.a forbids any \emph{new} candidate from reaching them.

\section{The proof graph (production layer)}\label{sec:proofgraph}
%======================================================================

The harness above operates on the Lemma~5.4 \emph{candidate workspace}
\code{ProofRefinement/Lemma54/}. Adjacent to it---and connected only
through file-based handoff---is the \emph{proof graph}, the production
trust boundary at \code{ProofGraph/}. The proof graph is a static DAG
of nodes representing each step in the end-to-end argument
$\Heta \to \text{Lemma 5.3} \to \text{Lemma 5.4} \to
\text{Theorem 5.1} \to \KG_{\text{lt}}^{\text{Krivine}}$. Each node has:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
  \item a Lean theorem name (the production-faithful statement),
  \item an \code{Effective} companion (instantiated at the locked tuple
        $\eta=1/100$, $r=23/25$),
  \item a \code{nodes/<name>.toml} declaring its trust dependencies and
        registered oracles,
  \item a runner-side certified-arb computation that emits a
        \code{certificate.json} cross-validating every oracle assumption.
\end{itemize}

The candidate workspace is a \emph{playground} for the harness; the
proof graph is the \emph{commitment surface}. A winning candidate is
ported to the proof graph by hand: a tightened formula becomes a
parallel \code{Cr_*} definition, a newly-proved analytic axiom drops
from the trust base, a tightened numerical bound is reflected in the
runner's certificate. This separation is by design: the harness can
run autonomously without touching the production trust boundary, and
only after a winning candidate has been cross-validated by hand does
its content move into \code{ProofGraph/}.

\section{The audit floor and PATCH\_NOTES}
%======================================================================

A subtle but important property of the harness is that retroactive
rule-tightening is supported through the
\code{PATCH_NOTES.md} mechanism. When a regression is discovered in the
trust base---e.g., the 2026-05-03 finding that every existing oracle
inherited a $K_{\max}$-truncated $\phi$-series with no rigorous tail
bound---the discovery is documented as a binding directive in
\code{PATCH_NOTES.md}: a new gate is added (G7), a parallel
truncation-free pipeline is built, a per-candidate audit re-evaluates
each $V_K$ under the post-patch oracles, and the resulting
$\Cpatched_K$ becomes the new floor.

The audit table records, for each $V_K$, three numbers: $\Cval_K$
(declared), $\Cpatched_K$ (production-evaluated under post-patch
oracles), and a structural label indicating which $\Cr$ formula and
oracle constellation $V_K$ uses. The table is the basis for the
\textbf{audit-floor consistency} gate G7.b: a new candidate
$V_{N+1}$'s audit-block header must name the cached audit JSON and the
specific $\Cpatched_{N+1}$ it claims, and the Evaluator rejects
$V_{N+1}$ if the audit JSON's $\Cpatched > \Cval$.

The take-away for the planner: a sprint cannot beat $\Cval$ by
literal-tightening alone. It must \emph{move the audit floor}, by
formula-switching, deeper subdivision, sharper joint oracles, or
alternative contours. Re-rationalising an oracle ceiling against a
non-rigorous floor is not a permitted direction.

%======================================================================
\section{Iteration loop and slash commands}
%======================================================================

A complete sprint, from the human's perspective, is a single slash
command:
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small,xleftmargin=2em]
/run-next-sprint
\end{Verbatim}
The skill spawns the \Role{Planner} with no arguments; it reads the
inputs above and writes \code{Sprint_Contracts/sprint_<NNN+1>.md}.
Then it spawns the \Role{Generator} with the contract path; the
Generator edits Lean and terminates with a labeled status. Then it
spawns the \Role{Evaluator} with the same contract path; the Evaluator
runs G1--G7 and writes a verdict. Total wall-clock: 5--30~minutes
depending on tier and Lean compile time.

For autonomous operation:
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small,xleftmargin=2em]
/loop 30m /run-next-sprint
\end{Verbatim}
The harness self-paces; \code{scripts/harness_preflight.sh} halts the
loop if the same goal fails three sprints in a row.

%======================================================================
\section{Safety properties}
%======================================================================

The harness is engineered around five guarantees, each enforced
mechanically by a different combination of file conventions and
gates.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0.3em]
  \item[(P1) The sink theorem statement is pinned.] $V_0$ declares
        \code{theorem lemma_5_4_search : <signature>}. Every later
        candidate must declare a theorem with the same name and
        statement (\code{lemma.toml:sink_theorem}). The Evaluator
        runs \code{#print axioms} on whatever the candidate names
        \code{lemma_5_4_search}; weakening the statement renders the
        \code{Cval} comparison meaningless and is treated as cheating
        by code review.
  \item[(P2) History is sacrosanct.] Older candidates and Sprint
        Contracts are immutable. Gate~G3 rejects any diff touching
        them. The Lean \code{olean} cache is wiped per sprint to
        avoid stale-binary leakage.
  \item[(P3) The trust base is on-paper.] \code{lemma.toml} is the
        only declaration of which seed axioms and which oracles a
        candidate may use. Any axiom outside this declaration is a
        gate~G4 failure, regardless of how it was introduced.
  \item[(P4) The trust base is re-checked every audit.] Every oracle
        is re-run on every G5; the Python implementation is the
        certificate. A bug in the implementation is caught by the
        bound-field-vs-recomputed-bound inequality.
  \item[(P5) Retroactive regression recovery is supported.] When a
        previously-trusted construction is found unsound (the
        $K_{\max}$ truncation), a new PATCH\_NOTES rule + new gate +
        new audit table re-classify every prior candidate. The
        framework does not ``patch in place''; the old cache stays,
        the new gate fails any future candidate that depends on the
        retired construction.
\end{description}

\section{Per-candidate proof graph visualisation}
%======================================================================

A complementary artifact, useful both for sanity-checking and for
paper-figure inclusion, is a \emph{per-candidate Lean dependency graph}:
for each $V_K$, the cone of \code{theorem}/\code{lemma}/\code{def}/\code{axiom}
declarations transitively reachable from \code{lemma_5_4_search} in
$V_K$ together with the seven shared
\code{Lemma54_Paper*.lean} library files.

\code{paper_artifacts/proof_graph/build_per_candidate.py} parses each
candidate, strips Lean comments to suppress false-positive
declarations, builds the directed graph by name-reference, restricts
to the cone reachable from the entry point, and renders with
Graphviz. Outputs:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
  \item \code{per_candidate/V<K>_proof_graph.{png,svg,pdf,dot}} --- one
        per candidate.
  \item \code{proof_graph_progression.gif} --- a 29-frame animation of
        the cone evolving across $V_0,\dots,V_{28}$.
  \item \code{proof_graph_progression.pdf} --- the same frames concatenated.
\end{itemize}
Node convention: \code{theorem}/\code{lemma} are rounded boxes,
\code{def} ellipses, \code{axiom} octagons; colour encodes the source

\section{Summary of the run that produced the bound}
%======================================================================

Across 34 sprints (29 candidates, several of which were
research-only or salvage runs), the certified $\Cval$ traced
\[
   8.2\!\times\!10^{14} \;\to\; 1.13\!\times\!10^{9} \;\to\; 3.79
   \;\to\; 1 \;\to\; 1/268{,}000
   \xrightarrow{K_{\max}\text{-patch}}
   0.3255 \;\to\; 0.2857.
\]
The trace shows three regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
  \item \emph{Numerical tightening of the paper formula}
        ($V_0\!\to\!V_8$): rectangle retargeting, $R$-rationalisation,
        oracle-precision bumps. Score moves $\sim 10^5\times$.
  \item \emph{Structural formula refinements} ($V_9\!\to\!V_{26}$): the
        auxiliary $z$-identity, joint sup-bounds replacing triangle
        products, the merged-numerator form, oracle-resolution
        scans. Score moves $\sim 10^{6}\times$ on the $K_{\max}$-era
        scale.
  \item \emph{Truncation-free re-baseline} ($V_{27}\!\to\!V_{28}$):
        post-PATCH\_NOTES, the score is now reported on the rigorous
        floor; the apparent regression to $0.33$ is the cost of
        retiring a non-rigorous oracle and the gain is that every
        future bound is honest at the $10^{-17}$ scale.
\end{enumerate}
The current best is $\Cval_{V_{28}} = 2/7$, against an audit floor
of $\Cpatched_{V_{28}} = 0.2810$ on $V_{14}$'s
$\Cr^{\phi,u_{zz},\text{joint}}$ formula at three rounds of corner
subdivision. The path forward, per
\code{PATCH_NOTES.md~§3a}, is to move the audit floor itself: a
fourth subdivision round, a fourth-order Taylor pad in $w$, a
sharper $M_{\Phi'''}$ Cauchy bound, or the wider analyticity domain
of \code{proposal_003}.
